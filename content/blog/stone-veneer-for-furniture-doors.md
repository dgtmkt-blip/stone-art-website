Not every surface you want in stone can carry the weight of stone. A wardrobe front, a door, a tabletop or a cabinet simply cannot hold a 20 mm slab. Flexible stone veneer solves that: a thin sheet of real stone on a flexible backing, light enough for furniture and strong enough to look right.

Industry sources describe flexible stone veneer at about 1.5 to 2 kg per square metre and 1.5 to 2 mm thick, applied to furniture surfaces the same way it goes on a wall. Here is how to use it well.

## Where veneer works on furniture

- **Wardrobe and cupboard fronts.** A single sheet across a wardrobe becomes a stone wall when the doors are closed.
- **TV and media units.** The whole TV wall, including the unit, reads as one continuous stone finish.
- **Headboards and bed walls.** A soft stone like limestone behind the bed, with lighting at the sides, gives a quiet, hotel-like feel.
- **Reception desks and bar fronts.** Front-of-house furniture that needs to look premium.
- **Door panels.** Entrance doors, pocket doors and sliding doors can carry stone where solid slab would be too heavy.
- **Cabinet doors in kitchens.** Lower cabinets or an island front, as a feature rather than every surface.
- **Dining and side tables.** Tabletops bonded to a solid substrate.
- **Shelving and alcove linings.** Stone backing to a bookshelf or display niche.

## Why it works

- **Light enough for furniture.** A 1 to 3 mm sheet does not strain hinges, drawers or sliding mechanisms.
- **Thin.** The sheet adds almost nothing to the thickness of the door or panel.
- **Flexible.** It can wrap curved fronts, soft corners and shaped edges.
- **Easy to cut.** A sharp utility knife or a saw with a diamond blade is enough for most cuts.
- **Real stone face.** The surface is natural stone, so each piece of furniture is unique.

## How to design with it

- **Pick one surface, not every surface.** A single stone wardrobe front against painted walls looks richer than a room where everything is stone.
- **Treat the edges.** Expose a clean cut edge for a modern look, or wrap it with a matching trim.
- **Let the stone take the attention.** Keep hardware simple on stone-faced doors, so the pattern does the talking.
- **Match the room.** The stone on a bed wall and the stone on a wardrobe can be the same sheet, which makes the room feel considered.

## Installation notes

Installing veneer on furniture is similar to installing it on a wall:

1. The substrate (MDF, plywood or a prepared door panel) must be clean, dry and flat.
2. Spread an even layer of a suitable adhesive on the back of the sheet.
3. Press the sheet onto the panel, from the centre outwards.
4. Trim the edges with a sharp knife.
5. Seal the surface with a penetrating stone sealer so marks come off easily.

Industry sources suggest a full installation often takes between 20 and 40 minutes per panel, depending on size.

## Common questions

### Can it be used on a kitchen worktop?

Flexible veneer is a surface layer, not a worktop material. It is thin and not meant to take knives, heat or heavy cookware. For worktops, use thicker stone or a worktop-rated surface. Veneer suits cabinet fronts and the island side, not the top.

### Does it survive wiping and cleaning?

Yes, with a sealer. Sealed stone resists water and most splashes. Wipe with a soft cloth and a mild cleaner; avoid acidic or abrasive cleaners.

### Can I use it on a door that gets a lot of use?

Yes. The sheet is well bonded and strong. On a hinge-side edge or a handle area, consider a trim or a cut-out so the sheet is not cut through daily.
