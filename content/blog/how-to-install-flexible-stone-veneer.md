Flexible stone veneer is one of the easier premium wall finishes to install. A good installer can cover a feature wall in a day. The method below follows standard industry practice and matches the general approach Stoneart recommends.

Always read the specific supplier's instructions for the product you have bought, and treat this as a plain-English overview, not a replacement for the installer's judgement on site.

## What you will need

- A clean, dry, flat wall of plaster, drywall, cement board or sound existing tile.
- Veneer sheets, cut to size.
- A tile adhesive the supplier recommends.
- A notched trowel (6 to 10 mm notches are typical).
- A sharp utility knife and a straight edge, or a circular saw or wet saw with a diamond blade for cleaner cuts.
- A spirit level and tape measure.
- Masking tape.
- A rubber roller or a soft block to press sheets flat.
- A clean cloth and a solvent recommended by the adhesive maker, to wipe off any squeeze-out before it sets.
- A penetrating stone sealer, if the product calls for one.

## Step 1: Prepare the surface

Check the wall is clean, dry, sound and flat. Industry installation guides are clear on this: adhesive bonds unevenly on uneven walls, and panels can lift or look crooked later.

- Scrape off loose paint and old adhesive.
- Fill cracks and holes, then sand smooth.
- Run a straight edge across the surface and feel for ridges or hollows. Fix anything you find.
- Make sure the wall is dry before you start.

## Step 2: Dry-fit the sheets

Hold each sheet against the wall before any adhesive goes on. Check the layout, the cuts and the match between sheets. Mark the pencil lines for cuts, then cut each sheet to size using a utility knife against a straight edge, or a saw with a diamond blade for cleaner edges.

Industry advice: measure twice, cut once. Wet cutting with a diamond blade keeps dust down and gives a cleaner edge.

## Step 3: Protect the edges

Run masking tape along the edges of each sheet before you spread adhesive. This small step keeps adhesive squeeze-out off the stone face, which is a lot easier than cleaning it off later.

## Step 4: Spread the adhesive

Mix the adhesive to the maker's instructions. Spread an even layer on the back of the sheet with a notched trowel, usually around 3 mm thick. Some installers also key the wall with a thin layer. The adhesive maker's instructions say which approach to use for the product.

Work in sections small enough that the adhesive stays workable for the whole sheet.

## Step 5: Press the sheet onto the wall

Place the sheet, align it, and press it firmly against the wall. Work from one edge to the other, pressing with the rubber roller or a soft block so there are no air pockets. Check with a spirit level that the sheet is straight.

For the next sheet, butt it tightly against the first so the joint is as tight as possible.

## Step 6: Clean up as you go

Wipe off any adhesive that squeezes through the joints before it sets. Use a clean cloth, dampened with a solvent the adhesive maker recommends.

## Step 7: Let it cure

Treat the wall gently for the first 24 to 48 hours while the adhesive dries. Industry guides give similar numbers: the wall is usable right away, but should not be loaded or wetted during the cure window.

## Step 8: Seal (if required)

For bathrooms, kitchens, outdoor walls and lighter stones, apply a penetrating stone sealer across the whole wall. Follow the sealer maker's instructions for how many coats and how long to leave between them. Reseal periodically; a common guide is every four years for interior use.

## Tips from the field

- **Mix sheets from several boxes.** Natural stone varies. Taking one sheet from each box and spreading them across the wall avoids a block of similar-looking sheets.
- **Mind the top edge.** A thin aluminium trim or a cut line hidden by a ceiling cornice looks neater than a raw edge.
- **Think about the first sheet.** It sets the alignment for the whole wall. Give it extra time.
- **Keep off-cuts.** They are useful for small patches around sockets, switches and future repairs.

## Common questions

### Can one person install it?

A single person can install a small feature wall. For taller walls, a second person makes alignment and holding sheets much easier.

### What if the wall is not perfectly flat?

Minor imperfections are hidden by the thickness of the adhesive. Larger ridges or hollows will show. Fix them first.

### Do I need to grout the joints?

No. Veneer sheets are usually butt-jointed with no grout. The joint lines read as natural seams in the stone.
